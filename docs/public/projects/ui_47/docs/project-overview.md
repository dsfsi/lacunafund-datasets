# Intraoperative Anesthesia and Outcomes Dataset

## Overview
This dataset can be used to identify patterns of intraoperative anesthesia practice and predict postoperative length of stay and risk of mortality based on intraoperative variables. It includes 2,066 intraoperative anesthesia records from two academic centers in sub-Saharan Africa.

## Dataset Information
- **Project ID**: 47
- **Domain**: Health
- **Data Type**: Clinical Dataset
- **Model Type**: Predictive Modeling
- **Dataset Size**: 2,066 intraoperative anesthesia records
- **License**: CC-BY 4.0 International

## Data Collection Methodology
The team employed innovative data collection techniques:
- Photographed completed intraoperative anesthesia records using smartphones
- De-identified all images for privacy protection
- Securely uploaded to HIPAA-compliant servers
- Used combination of computer vision AI and manual extraction techniques

## Comprehensive Data Elements
The dataset includes the following comprehensive intraoperative data:
- **Demographic data**: Patient characteristics and basic information
- **Medication data**: Anesthetic agents and dosages administered
- **Hemodynamic data**: Blood pressure, heart rate, and circulation metrics
- **Physiological data**: Respiratory parameters and vital signs
- **Anesthesia type**: Regional, general, or combined techniques
- **Surgery type**: Surgical procedure classifications
- **Postoperative length of stay**: Recovery time measurements
- **30-day postoperative mortality**: Short-term outcome tracking

## Use Cases
- Mortality risk prediction modeling
- Postoperative recovery time prediction
- Anesthesia practice pattern analysis
- Clinical decision support system development
- Quality improvement initiatives
- Resource allocation planning

## Research Significance
This dataset fills a critical gap in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) where:
- Electronic intraoperative anesthesia datasets are limited in scope
- Key data elements are often missing or unavailable
- Comprehensive clinical data is essential for improving patient outcomes
- Evidence-based practice development is needed

## Geographic Coverage
- **Region**: Sub-Saharan Africa
- **Setting**: Two academic medical centers
